A flock of starlings at dusk spirals into a vortex without a single collision — so particles in a collisionless plasma obey the Vlasov equation. Derived in 1938, it generalizes the Boltzmann equation to the case of dominant collective interactions. The distribution function f(t, x, v) evolves under the action of self-consistent fields E and B, determined through charge and current densities by Maxwell's equations. The system is nonlinear and describes effects of Landau damping, instabilities, and waves in plasma. Each bird sensitively responds to the overall rhythm, silently changing its trajectory.

Discovery

Anatoly Alexandrovich Vlasov in 1938 published the work 'On the Vibrational Properties of an Electron Gas', where he proposed the collisionless equation. The idea met with resistance — Landau, Tamm, and others criticized the lack of accounting for collisions. Nevertheless, the equation became a standard in plasma physics, and later in astrophysics for modeling gravitating systems. In 1945, Vlasov generalized it to the case of solids.

How it works

Self-consistent solution: the distribution function determines the sources of fields, the fields determine the evolution of the distribution. Used in numerical PIC (particle-in-cell) codes for modeling laser plasma, solar wind, galaxy dynamics. Applicability limit: valid when the collision frequency is much less than the plasma frequency; at not too high densities. For collisional plasma, a collision operator is necessary.

Caveats

attention mechanism

5

A spotlight on stage picks out the main actor at each moment of dialogue. The rest temporarily recedes into shadow.

In practice: Smart cameras that track motion, and translation systems that grasp the meaning of a long phrase, work exactly like that.
The attention mechanism is a computational technique that allows a model to dynamically weigh the importance of different parts of the input data. Unlike fixed convolutions, attention adapts to each specific example: the word "crane" (water tap or construction machine) will get different surroundings depending on the context. Like a director moving the spotlight from one actor to another, attention redirects light according to the mise-en-scène.

History

The idea of "attention" in machine learning was proposed in the 1990s, and in 2014–2015 working models for translation appeared (Bahdanau et al.). Later it became a key part of transformers.

How it works

Each word, as it were, asks all the others: "How important are you to me right now?" The answer is a weight (a numerical score). Then all the words are mixed in the right proportion, creating a refined representation for that position in the sentence.

💡 The human visual system also has an analogue — selective attention: the brain amplifies the signal from the desired object and suppresses the rest.
A mechanism that, when processing data, can focus on important details, like a spotlight on stage. When reading a sentence, for each word it determines which other words to pay attention to in order to understand the meaning more precisely. As a result, a new picture is created where the connections between words are highlighted — like a stage where all actors are visible at once, but the lead actor stands out brighter.

The attention mechanism in neural networks computes a context-dependent representation of a sequence element by aggregating information from all elements with weights that depend on their compatibility. Mathematically, it is a weighted sum of values, where the weights are obtained as the similarity of queries and keys. A generalization is self-attention, where queries, keys, and values are generated from the same source. A theatrical lighting instrument does not just shine uniformly — it emphasizes dramatic moments. Similarly, attention highlights semantic centers in the input stream, allowing the model to build a hierarchy of significance. The result: instead of a flat sequence, a volumetric understanding emerges, where each element is connected to the others by a dynamic web of beams.

History

The concept of attention in AI goes back to ideas from the 1960s about visual search. In neural networks, the first implementations were alignment systems for machine translation (Bahdanau et al., 2014). Then the mechanism became modular with "Attention is All You Need" (2017), where it was transformed into a self-contained building block — self-attention in the transformer.

How it works

Three matrices are computed: queries Q, keys K, values V (linear projections of the inputs). For each query, dot products are calculated with all keys, scaled by √d_k, and transformed via softmax into a probability distribution. These probabilities are used as weights for summing the values. Multi-head attention repeats the procedure several times with different projections, capturing various types of relationships.

\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
Q - queries, K - keys, V - values, d_k - dimension of keys, softmax - function returning probabilities, √d_k - scaling factor to prevent large softmax arguments

Open problems & fun facts

Quadratic growth of computational complexity with sequence length (the problem of long documents); Explainability: why did the model focus specifically on these elements?; Plasticity: how to maintain the ability to learn quickly without forgetting?

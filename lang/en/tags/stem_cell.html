stem cell

A creator cell that can turn into other cells of the body, like clay from which any shapes can be molded.

In practice: Stem cells repair damaged tissues and organs — they are used to treat leukemia, burns, and test new drugs.
Stem cells are distinguished by two properties: self-renewal (unlimited division without loss of potential) and potency — the ability to differentiate into specialized types. Totipotent cells (zygote) can create an entire organism; pluripotent (embryonic) — all tissues except extraembryonic membranes; multipotent (adult) — a limited set of cells. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) are obtained by activating certain genes in mature cells.

History

In the 1960s, Ernest McCulloch and James Till discovered hematopoietic stem cells in mice. Then human embryonic stem cells were isolated (1998), and in 2006 Shinya Yamanaka created induced stem cells by reprogramming ordinary adult cells.

How it works

When dividing, stem cells create one twin copy (self-renewal) and one cell that begins to specialize under the influence of chemical signals. For example, in the bone marrow, signals cause descendants to become red or white blood cells.

💡 More than 80 diseases, including some types of cancer and anemia, are successfully treated with umbilical cord blood stem cells.
Stem cells are cells without a specialty but with an outstanding ability: they can divide and become any other cells of the body (neurons, muscles, bones). There are two main types: embryonic (obtained from early embryos — embryos at an early stage of development), which can give rise to a whole organism, and adult (in bone marrow, skin), which repair individual damages. Just as a cook can make both bread and pancakes from one dough, a stem cell generates different tissues.

Stem cells are characterized by asymmetric division, in which one daughter cell retains stemness and the other enters the path of differentiation. Pluripotent stem cells maintain the expression of key transcription factors OCT4, SOX2, NANOG, forming an autoregulatory network. Their differentiation into three germ layers (ectoderm, mesoderm, endoderm) and further into specialized cells is regulated by signaling pathways (Wnt, BMP, Notch, FGF). The microenvironment (niche) through extracellular matrix components and paracrine factors determines the balance between quiescence, self-renewal, and commitment. The iPSC technology (Shinya Yamanaka, 2006) is based on transient overexpression of the genes Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, c-Myc, reverting somatic cells to a pluripotent state. Hematopoietic stem cells are clinically used for bone marrow transplantation, and methods of cell therapy for regeneration of myocardium, neurons, and pancreatic β-cells are being developed.

History

The idea of stem cells dates back to the work of Ernst Haeckel (1868) and August Weismann (1885). Experimental confirmation was provided by Canadian hematologists Ernest McCulloch and James Till, who discovered spleen colony-forming units — hematopoietic stem cells of the mouse in 1963. The isolation of human embryonic stem cells by Martin Evans (1981 for mouse, 1998 for human) and the creation of induced pluripotent stem cells by Shinya Yamanaka (2006, Nobel Prize 2012) opened the era of regenerative medicine.

How it works

The molecular machinery of pluripotency includes mutually reinforcing transcription factors OCT4, SOX2, NANOG, which bind their own promoters and each other's promoters, and also repress differentiation genes through recruitment of polycomb repressor complexes. Signaling molecules of the niche (SCF, Notch ligands, Wnt) activate intracellular cascades that alter the epigenetic landscape of DNA methylation and histone modifications, switching the cell from a naive to a primed state of pluripotency. Differentiation proceeds as a sequential loss of potencies with parallel activation of tissue-specific genes. Upon transplantation, stem cells are subject to the risk of teratoma formation, therefore methods of directed in vitro differentiation with subsequent purification of target populations are being developed.

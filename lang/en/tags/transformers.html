The Transformer is a neural network architecture where the entire sequence is processed in parallel. The word “bank” (meaning a financial institution or a bench) clarifies its meaning through connections with other words in the sentence, using the attention mechanism (dynamic importance weighting). This overcame the limitations of older models that got confused in long phrases. Similarly, a command center distributes tasks: each agent sees the big picture and identifies priority targets.

History

First described in 2017 by the "Attention Is All You Need" team. It replaced previous approaches (recurrent networks that read text like a tape — letter by letter).

How it works

Instead of reading sequentially, the Transformer looks at the entire sentence at once. Each word sends queries to others (“what is important for me here?”), receives responses, and refines its meaning considering the context. Thus, several layers in succession build a complete picture.

The Transformer is a neural network architecture for processing sequences, based solely on the attention mechanism. By forgoing recurrent and convolutional layers, it uses multi-head self-attention, allowing each element of the sequence to interact with all others simultaneously. The architecture consists of an encoder and decoder with positional encoding, normalization layers, and residual connections. Scalability has enabled the creation of giant models that compress knowledge about the world into weights. Imagine a search party in a vast forest. Instead of relaying information through a chain, each scout instantly shares observations with everyone else. The Transformer does the same: words cease to be isolated units and form a unified map of meanings. In the end, the squad gathers at headquarters with a complete understanding of the situation — just as the model produces a holistic representation of the sentence.

History

Proposed in the paper "Attention is All You Need" (2017) by Vaswani and colleagues at Google. The core idea of the attention mechanism dates back to work in the 1990s, but the Transformer established it as a self-sufficient building block. It first conquered machine translation, then generative models (GPT, BERT), computer vision, and even protein structure prediction.

How it works

The key element is self-attention: for the input sequence, matrices of queries (Q), keys (K), and values (V) are computed. Attention weights are obtained as the scaled dot product of queries and keys, followed by softmax. Formula: Attention(Q,K,V) = softmax(QK^T/√d_k)V, where d_k is the dimension of the keys. The encoder applies multi-head attention: several parallel heads project the input into different subspaces, then the results are concatenated. Positional encoding adds information about word order, since the model has no built-in notion of sequence.

\text{Attention}(Q, K, V) = \text{softmax}\left(\frac{QK^T}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)V
Q is the query matrix, K is the key matrix, V is the value matrix, d_k is the dimension of the keys (usually 64), softmax is a function that converts numbers into a probability distribution along the last axis, T denotes transposition.

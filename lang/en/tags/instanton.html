instanton

The quantum vacuum is not empty—fleeting clumps of energy swarm within it. An instanton is one such clump, living only a moment but changing the rules of the game.

In practice: Calculations with instantons explain why neutrons and protons hold together so tightly, and help understand how the void is structured at the micro level.
Instantons are non-perturbative solutions of field equations in Euclidean spacetime. They describe tunneling transitions between vacuum states of gauge theories, such as quantum chromodynamics. An instanton is localized: its action is concentrated in a limited region and over a short time interval, hence the name (from instant). Summing over instantons provides a contribution to physical quantities that is inaccessible through ordinary perturbation theory.

History

In the 1970s, theorists applied this idea to the strong interaction to figure out why quarks do not fly apart.

How it works

Imagine a ball in a hollow. Classically, it cannot roll out. But quantum mechanically, it can 'seep' through the wall. The instanton calculates how often this happens, using a clever trick with imaginary time.

💡 It is instantons that are responsible for the η' meson being noticeably heavier than other light mesons.
In the quantum world, particles can pass through barriers—this is the tunneling effect. An instanton is a convenient way to calculate the probability of such an event. To do this, physicists 'rotate' time into an imaginary plane, and the barrier disappears. An instanton looks like a burst of field localized in time and space.

An instanton is a classical solution of field equations in Euclidean spacetime (with metric ++++) with finite action, interpolating between topologically distinct vacua in non-Abelian gauge theories. In quantum chromodynamics (QCD), instantons represent tunneling transitions between states with different values of the topological charge Q = (g²/32π²) ∫ G∧G̃. They provide a non-perturbative contribution to the path integral, resolving the U_A(1) anomaly problem and explaining the large mass of the η' meson. Instantons play a role in confinement, the formation of the quark condensate, and spontaneous chiral symmetry breaking.

History

The concept of the instanton emerged in 1975 in the works of Alexander Polyakov and collaborators, but its roots go back to the study of tunneling in double-well quantum mechanics. In 1976, Gerard 't Hooft computed the instanton-induced interaction of quarks. The solution of the Yang-Mills equations in Euclidean space had been found earlier—the Belavin-Polyakov-Tyupkin instanton (BPST, 1975). In the 1980s, instanton methods were applied to electroweak theory, where they predict baryon number violation.

How it works

The instanton action S = 8π²/g², where g is the gauge coupling constant. The tunneling amplitude is proportional to e^{-S}, so at small g the instanton contribution is exponentially suppressed, but becomes significant at large g (in the infrared regime of QCD). In the path integral, transitioning to Euclidean time (τ = it) turns oscillating exponentials into decaying ones, making the tunneling path the 'most probable'. The instanton solution is self-dual: F = ±*F, which guarantees saturation of the Bogomolny bound and minimum action. For calculations, the 't Hooft ansatz is used, summing configurations with many instantons and anti-instantons (instanton gas). The tunneling effect is described similarly, but with imaginary time.

S = \frac{8\pi^2}{g^2}
S — Euclidean action, g — gauge coupling constant (dimensionless, for QCD g ~ 1 at the confinement scale)

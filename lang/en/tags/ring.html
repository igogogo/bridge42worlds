Tag

ring

Integers can be added and multiplied, and the result always remains an integer. A ring generalizes this idea to other worlds — from ornament patterns to programming languages.

In practice: Cryptosystems that protect your messages in messengers are built on polynomial rings.
In mathematics, a ring is a set on which two associative operations are defined, usually called addition and multiplication. Addition must form an abelian group (there is a zero and subtraction), and multiplication is distributive over addition (a⋅(b + c) = a⋅b + a⋅c). Typical rings: integers ℤ, polynomials in one variable ℤ[x], square matrices of order n. If multiplication is commutative, the ring is called commutative. Ring theory studies ideals — subsets that resemble 'multiples of a given number', and their properties, which allows solving equations in integers and classifying algebraic objects.

History

Emmy Noether in the 1920s turned scattered examples into a coherent theory of rings, showing that many problems in number theory and geometry speak the same language — the language of ideals and quotient rings.

How it works

Rings appear in situations with cyclicity and combination rules. The twelve notes in an octave form a ring: transposition by a semitone adds 1 modulo 12, and because of this, chords and harmonies are possible. The rules of the ring guarantee that musical arithmetic never leaves the octave.

💡 In the ring of even numbers, there is no identity (neutral element for multiplication) because 1 is odd. Nevertheless, it is a fully-fledged ring with its own ideals.
A ring is a set with two operations: 'addition' and 'multiplication'. Addition can always be reversed (subtraction), but multiplication not always (not every element has an 'inverse'). Familiar examples: integers, where 3 times 5 equals 15, but dividing 15 by 2 leaves a remainder. Another example: rotations of a square: two consecutive rotations give a new rotation, and 'adding' rotations means performing one after another. Such a structure helps describe symmetries.

A ring (R, +, ·) is an algebraic structure where (R, +) is an abelian group (with zero 0), (R, ·) is a semigroup (multiplication is associative), and distributivity holds: a·(b + c) = a·b + a·c and (b + c)·a = b·a + c·a. If there exists an element 1 ≠ 0 such that 1·a = a·1 = a for all a, the ring is called a ring with identity. Commutative rings with identity and without zero divisors are integral domains; an example is ℤ. A field is a ring in which every non-zero element is invertible under multiplication. Ring theory deals with ideals, modules, and homomorphisms, and the spectrum of a ring connects algebra with topology.

History

The term 'Zahlring' was introduced by David Hilbert in 1897 in the context of rings of integers in algebraic number fields. Systematic development of general ring theory was carried out by Emmy Noether in her work 'Idealtheorie in Ringbereichen' (1921), where she established conditions for chain termination of ideals and their connection with decomposition into primes. Axiomatization of the definition of a ring was completed by the 1930s through the works of van der Waerden and other algebraists.

How it works

Rings serve as a language for describing extensions of numbers. The residue ring ℤ/nℤ models integer arithmetic modulo n, which underlies RSA encryption. The polynomial ring k[x1,…,xn] over a field k corresponds to affine space 𝔸ⁿ, and its quotient rings describe algebraic varieties. In quantum mechanics, self-adjoint operators on a Hilbert space form a Jordan ring, and measurable classes form a ring with convergence closure. Endomorphism rings of abelian groups reveal deep connections between linear algebra and homological algebra.

a \cdot (b + c) = a \cdot b + a \cdot c

Open problems & fun facts

Burnside problem for rings: is a ring finite if it is generated by a finite number of nil elements and satisfies the identity xⁿ = 0?; Classification of division rings of finite dimension over their center (division algebras); Nakai conjecture: every finitely generated algebra with identity that is a finite type module over its center is isomorphic to a matrix ring over a commutative ring.

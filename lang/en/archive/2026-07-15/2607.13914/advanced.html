Epidemic Under Control: A Recipe for Systemic Approach express

Original: "Epidemic Informatics and Control: A Holistic Approach from System Informatics to Epidemic Response and Risk Management in Public Health"
· Hui Yang, Siqi Zhang, Runsang Liu, Alexander Krall, Yidan Wang, Marta Ventura, Chris Deflitch
arXiv:2607.13914 · 2026-07-15 · CC BY · 1 min
eess.SY cs.CY cs.SY Applied Statistics
The DMAIC method is like a plumber's guide for epidemics: a clear plan from chaos to control.
Abstract

A systems informatics approach DMAIC (Define, Measure, Analyze, Improve, Control) is presented for epidemic response, intensively leveraging data, statistics, and optimization. The Define stage examines challenges for public health, healthcare systems, and sectors (retail, education, manufacturing). The Measure stage includes an overview of medical testing, statistical sampling, data management, and visualization. Analyze aims to extract insights and support decisions using analytical tools. Improve covers telemedicine, AI, resource reallocation, and system redesign. Control involves predictive policy optimization methods to contain the virus. This work seeks to stimulate deeper interdisciplinary research to apply systems informatics in epidemics.

When a pipe bursts in the basement, a plumber acts step by step: locates the leak, measures the pressure, determines the cause, patches it up, and checks if it's dry. The same recipe — the standard model DMAIC — is being proposed against epidemics.

First, they determine the scope — how many hospital beds are occupied, which industries are sagging. Then comes data collection: surveys, tests, statistics. Just as a plumber listens to the pipes and watches the drips, scientists track outbreaks. Markovian models predict the spread of infection before it's too late to reinforce weak points: move appointments online, redistribute doctors, boost the system's flexibility.

The finale is control: precise calculation of measures to slow the epidemic without unnecessary lockdowns. It's surprising that DMAIC was honed on Motorola's factory assembly lines — there it weeded out defects and saved billions. Now it's fixing not pipes, but society. An epidemic increases entropy, and a systems approach, much like the ideas of von Neumann in computing, restores structure.

🎯 Interestingly, the first tests of DMAIC in healthcare helped cut hospital queues in half — without extra funding.

🎬 In the movie 'Contagion' (2011), panic hinders the search for a vaccine; the DMAIC protocol, like a plumber's blueprint, would have brought clarity.

AI Judges Listen Not with Ears, but with Labels express

Original: "Auditing Protocol-Level Shortcuts in Large Audio Language Model Judges for Speech Evaluation"
· Joonyong Park, David M. Chan, Yuki Saito, Hiroshi Saruwatari
arXiv:2607.13477 · 2026-07-15 · CC BY-SA 4.0 · 1 min
cs.SD Computation and Language eess.AS
AI speech assessment systems often rely on textual cues rather than the sound itself.
Abstract

Large audio-language models (LALMs) are deployed as automatic judges for speech evaluation, but high correlation with human ratings does not mean verdicts are based on audio. An audit of protocol 'shortcuts' was conducted in three typical protocols: feature-blueprint judging (judgment from textual descriptions of acoustic features), reference-based judging, and pairwise A/B comparison. Across six judges and four attributes, many models were found to exploit shortcuts. For instance, when audio is replaced with a structured text description containing deliberately incorrect labels, five models' emotion assessment accuracy drops to 0.10 or below. In concatenated A/B comparisons, Qwen3-Omni-Thinking often selects the same slot regardless of sample order. Results indicate that aggregate agreement metrics can overestimate judgment reliability unless the model and protocol are analyzed jointly; a dedicated shortcut probe is necessary for each model-protocol pair.

Automatic speech judges are algorithms that evaluate pronunciation and emotions. However, they often work like a food critic who delivers a verdict after reading the menu but not tasting the dish.

Instead of analyzing the sound, the model grabs textual cues—like judging lasagna by the recipe rather than the taste.

Experiments showed that simply swapping two audio recordings makes the AI judge change its score, even though the sound remains the same. It latches onto the order of presentation, not the actual quality. Relying on the standard protocol of testing, rather than the true sound, such systems cannot be considered fair. To trust them in the future, every model must be checked for a tendency to cheat—otherwise, the evaluation will remain a fiction.

🎯 A neural network can give different scores to the same recording just by swapping the order of samples.

Neural Network Antibiotic Resistance Detector express

Original: "Screening of Biosecurity Features in Metagenomic Data with Evo 2 Probes"
· Jeremy Guntoro, Alexander Dack, Dylan Danno, Michaela Jančovičová, Križan Jurinović, Vanessa Smilansky
arXiv:2607.14070 · 2026-07-15 · CC BY 4.0 · 1 min
q-bio.GN Machine Learning
Scientists taught a neural network to quickly find dangerous genes in bacteria without retraining it.
Abstract

Genomic foundation models, such as Evo 2, learn rich sequence representations, but their applicability for biosecurity screening is underexplored. Linear and attention probes were trained on frozen layer 26 activations of Evo 2, without model fine-tuning. On metagenomic tests, probes detect antimicrobial resistance (AMR) genes: the linear probe achieves a regional ROC-AUC of 0.888 (mean-pool), and the attention probe — 0.977. They distinguish AMR subclasses and separate them from unrelated genes, confirming signal specificity. Virulence is decoded more weakly (0.833). The AMR probe maintains effectiveness on simulated short reads without retraining (read-level ROC-AUC 0.898), enabling pre-assembly assessment. On sequences generated by Evo 1.5, labels are recovered weakly; a sparse autoencoder reveals features but less stably. The results present lightweight probes over embeddings as a fast and inexpensive first detection layer for metagenomic biosurveillance.

Neural networks trained on genetic text can 'read' the DNA of bacteria. Scientists took the powerful Evo 2 model and added a tiny, tunable sensor—a genetic probe—without touching the model itself. The probe picks up signals associated with dangerous genes, like antibiotic resistance. Similar to spectroscopy—a method of analyzing substances by their characteristic glow—the probe recognizes the model's specific 'response' to a genetic sequence.

The probe works like a metal detector at an airport: it doesn't need to know the entire contents of a suitcase, just notice a characteristic signal

This approach turned out to be surprisingly accurate. Even a simple probe gives high results, and with the addition of an attention mechanism, accuracy reaches almost 0.99—it can distinguish subtypes of drug resistance. And, like a metal detector, it handles fragments of DNA. This allows cheap and fast testing of hospital wastewater and hidden sewer threats, warning of infection outbreaks before symptoms appear in people.

🎯 Antibiotic resistance is one of the major health threats: by 2050, it could kill 10 million people annually.

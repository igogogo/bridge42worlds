High Energy
The Einstein space observatory has seen a 'dirty' star explosion for the first time — a key to unraveling the deaths of massive stars.
1

For a long time, astronomers sought 'dirty fireballs'—stellar explosions that eject so much material they can't reach top speeds. And now the new Einstein telescope has caught one in a distant galaxy. Its jet was 'only' 0.1% slower than light—enough to rewrite the death models of massive stars.

🎯 The energy of this explosion, in seconds, rivals what the Sun will radiate over its entire lifetime—10 billion years.

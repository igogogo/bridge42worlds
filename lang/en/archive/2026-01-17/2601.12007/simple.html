Star Dough: How Dust Tells the Age of Newborn Stars express

Original: "The identification of new Herbig Ae/Be stars from LAMOST DR7"
· Jialin Liu, Jiaming Liu, Jiya Yao, Zhenghao Cheng, Qingyue Qu, Zhicun Liu, Wenyuan Cui, Min Fang
arXiv:2601.12007 · 2026-01-17 · CC BY · 1 min
Stellar Galaxies
Astronomers found 26 stellar 'infants' and, by the hue of their dust, built a maturity scale—from sticky dough to golden crust.
Abstract

Scientists sifted through millions of stellar spectra to spot rare 'teenage heavyweights' — Herbig Ae/Be stars. They discovered 26 new objects and created a diagram that traces their growing-up stages, like height marks on a door frame. Imagine: by a star’s color, you can tell how much it’s still 'swaddled' in gas and dust!

1

The birth of a star is like cooking in a giant cosmic oven. First, an icy cloud of cosmic dust and gas contracts, and a thermonuclear fire ignites in the center, but it's still hidden behind a dense layer of 'flour'. It can only be seen in thermal rays—the dust heats up and glows, giving away the core.

Astronomers examined the rainbow fingerprints of light from thousands of stars, looking for the glow of hydrogen—a sign that a reaction is happening inside. That's how they found 26 previously unknown 'dusty buns'. The key discovery is a color scale: by matching brightness data in different infrared tones, scientists traced a path from an object thickly coated in dough to a golden star with a crispy crust.

These stars are 2–10 times heavier than the Sun. Their dust disks are the cradles of future planets.

The biggest surprise: the diagram has a gap—a stage where the dust disappears so rapidly that we hardly have time to notice it, like a biscuit that burns almost instantly in grill mode.

🎯 The color diagram revealed a gap: a stage where the dust disappears almost instantly. These stars are so rare, it's as if nature switches the oven from 'bake' to 'grill'.

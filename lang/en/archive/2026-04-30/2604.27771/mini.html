The Universe's Size and Expansion Are Uncertain express

Original: "A Cosmological Uncertainty Relation and Late-Universe Acceleration"
· Savvas M. Koushiappas
arXiv:2604.27771 · 2026-04-30 · CC BY 4.0 · 1 min
Cosmology General Relativity HEP Phenomenology HEP Theory
A new quantum rule says: you can't simultaneously measure the size of the Universe and its expansion rate.
1

It turns out you can't precisely measure the Universe's size and its expansion rate at the same time—quantum uncertainty gets in the way. This isn't just philosophy; it's a concrete model that, for different parameter values, explains dark energy or even eliminates the Big Bang, replacing it with a smooth bounce. And no new particles needed.

🎯 If the Universe is an inflating balloon, quantum effects will necessarily blur either its size or its expansion rate—you can't get precise values for both at once.

🎬 The model echoes the idea of a cyclic Universe from science fiction: the cosmos doesn't explode but smoothly transitions from contraction to expansion.

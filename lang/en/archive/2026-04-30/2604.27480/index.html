Magnetic Footprints in the Glow of a Distant Explosion express

Original: "First Detection of Faraday Rotation in a Gamma-Ray Burst Afterglow: Low Polarization and High Rotation Measure in GRB 260310A Reveal Jet Magnetic Structure and Environment"
· Collin T. Christy, Tanmoy Laskar, Kate D. Alexander, Noah Franz, Jonathan Granot, Ryan Chornock, Raffaella Margutti, Ramandeep Gill, Jeniveve Pearson, Edo Berger, Wen-fai Fong, Coleman Rohde, Patricia Schady
arXiv:2604.27480 · 2026-04-30 · CC BY 4.0 · 1 min
High Energy
The polarization caught in the radio afterglow of a gamma-ray burst allowed astronomers to peer into the magnetic environment of the explosion.
Abstract

For the first time, linear polarization of radio emission at centimeter wavelengths and the Faraday effect (rotation of polarization in a magnetic field) have been detected from a gamma-ray burst. The polarization fraction decreased from 3.2% at 25 GHz to 0.7% at 11 GHz — the suppression at lower frequencies is due to synchrotron self-absorption, while the low values at higher frequencies arise from the inhomogeneous magnetic field of the jet. The rotation measure RM = –8300 rad/m² indicates that the signal passed through a dense ionized nebula (HII region) around the progenitor star. These polarimetric measurements open the door to studying in detail the evolution of magnetic fields in the afterglows of gamma-ray bursts.

Radio waves from a gamma-ray burst—the death of a massive star, briefly outshining its galaxy—arrive at us polarized, oscillating in a single plane, like a compass needle frozen in a magnetic field. Astronomers have read the indications of this cosmic needle for the first time.

Using spectroscopy, they traced how polarization increased with frequency: 0.7% at 11 GHz, 3.2% at 25 GHz. But even at its peak, it's far from perfect—the magnetic field in the ejected jet turned out not uniform, but made of many chaotic domains, like thousands of microscopic compasses pointing every which way.

A staggering detail: on the way to Earth, the polarization rotated by –8300 rad/m²—tens of thousands of times stronger than in our Galaxy. This Faraday rotation reveals a dense veil of ionized hydrogen around the explosion site, like a magnetic lens. Now, by tracking the dance of this needle, scientists will be able to peer into the very heart of cosmic cataclysms.

🎯 Each 'patch' of the tangled magnetic field in the jet is so tiny that from Earth it appears as a speck no larger than the Moon.

There Is No Universal Map for All Groups

Original: "Obstructions to coarse universality for finitely generated groups"
· Robin Tucker-Drob
arXiv:2607.00963 · 2026-07-01 · CC BY · 4 min
math.GR math.MG
For any bounded-degree graph with a finite-cococompact coarse action, there exists a finitely generated group that cannot be coarsely embedded into it.
Abstract

It is proved that for any graph Λ of bounded degree that admits a finitely cocompact coarse quasi-action of some group, there exists a finitely generated group that does not coarsely embed into Λ. More generally, for any countable family of such graphs, there is a finitely generated group that does not coarsely embed into any of them. This settles two conjectures of Simon Thomas: there is neither a universal Cayley graph nor a universal quasi-isometry class of finitely generated groups. Consequently, no second-countable locally compact group coarsely contains all finitely generated groups. The proof uses an exponential upper bound on the number of finite graphs that admit an (L,M)-regular map into Λ, and a superexponential stock of trivalent graphs of large girth, yielding a sequence of finite obstructing graphs. Then, via graphical small-cancellation techniques—based on a variant of Osajda's theorem developed by Esperet and Ciocanti—this sequence is isometrically realized inside the Cayley graph of some finitely generated group.

1

Context

On the table lies a huge sheet of paper. A cartographer tries to put every conceivable landscape onto it: mountain ridges, tangled ravines, vast plains. Each landscape is a finitely generated group. The map is a Cayley graph with a fixed vertex degree. Distortions are allowed: distances can be stretched and squeezed, but only up to a fixed factor. Simon Thomas formulated the conjectures: is it possible to create such a universal map? Tucker-Drob’s theorem answers: no—regardless of the scale and the allowed coarse distortions, some landscapes will always fail to fit on the table.

Methods

To prove this, the mathematician splits the problem into stages. First, fix a target map Λ—a bounded-degree graph with a finite-cococompact coarse quasi-action, meaning it has a finite set of “typical” sheets that can be combined to approximate any point. If every group could be placed on Λ, then there would exist regular maps—L-Lipschitz maps with fiber size at most M. Using the quasi-action, the image of the map is nudged toward a fixed finite set of landmarks, and after exploring possible preimages in the lexicographic product, one obtains an exponential upper bound on the number of isomorphism classes of graphs on s vertices that can be embedded in Λ: at most A^s. In parallel, the Linial–Simkin theorem provides a stash of 3-regular landscapes with long cycles without self-intersections (girth ≥ c log_2 s), and these landscapes are super-exponentially plentiful—at least (a s)^{3s/2}. For large s, the super-exponential overtakes the exponential, yielding an obstruction landscape that cannot be drawn on Λ without exceeding the distortion limit. Varying parameters (L,M) and running through a countable family of target maps, one builds a sequence of such obstructions with rapidly increasing girth and a bounded ratio of ridges to cycle length. Then graphical small cancellation comes to the rescue—a method of labeling edges with symbols from a finite alphabet so that long paths do not repeat (a variant of Osajda’s theorem, implemented by Espère and Gocanti). This coded labeling allows all obstruction landscapes to be glued into a single giant group K, whose Cayley graph contains them isometrically.

Results

The main result: for any sequence of maps Λ_i with a finite number of typical sheets, there exists a finitely generated obstruction group K that cannot be coarsely transferred onto any Λ_i. Consequently, there is no universal graph-map—Thomas’s conjectures are refuted. No locally compact group with a countable base can serve as a coarse container for all finitely generated groups. Moreover, such obstruction groups can be built in a continuum, pairwise incomparable by quasi-isometry. Numerically, the clash between exponential and super-exponential becomes decisive already at moderate sizes s; the constant A grows with the required map precision.

Implications

The result dashes the hope for a unified coarse classification of all groups and forces us to study landscapes one by one. The cartographer’s sheet of paper remains incomplete: some reliefs will always fall beyond its edges. Methodologically, the synthesis of graph counting and small cancellation provides a powerful tool for constructing exotic spaces. For dynamical systems theory, it underscores that even regular actions with finitely many orbits do not exhaust the diversity of finitely generated groups.

Future development

Naturally, one seeks quantitative strengthenings: how fast must the parameters grow before an obstruction appears? Can one relax the quasi-action condition to merely bounded degree and the absence of universality? Exploring coarse universality in other categories—for instance, for locally compact groups with weaker conditions—is also of interest.

Impact

On geometric group theory, coarse geometry, topology, and dynamical systems theory.

Next steps

Investigating optimal constants in the exponential bound and searching for explicit constructive obstructions without a diagonal argument. Systematic study of coarse universality in classes of locally compact groups.

Key open problems

The theorem settles two questions from the program of classifying finitely generated groups up to quasi-isometry, which goes back to Gromov. It also contributes to the problem of the existence of universal objects in categories with coarse morphisms.

Key numbers

  • exponential base A: depends on embedding parameters, on the order of thousands
  • super-exponential growth exponent: 3/2
  • small cancellation parameter λ: ≤ 1/6
  • degree of obstruction graphs: 3
  • minimum girth of an obstruction graph: c log_2 s, c ∈ (0,1)

The Chosen Coalitions Club: Inside von Neumann and Morgenstern Stability

Original: "A characterization of the von Neumann and Morgenstern stable set in matching markets"
· Lucero Quevedo Mauricio, Paola Manasero, Pablo Neme, Jorge Oviedo
arXiv:2607.00869 · 2026-07-01 · CC0 · 3 min
econ.TH
In one-sided markets, the unique von Neumann–Morgenstern stable set exactly matches the core of the reduced preference profile.
Abstract

We study the structure and computation of vNM-stable sets in one-to-one matching markets. With strict preferences, pairwise and core stability coincide and are well understood; vNM stability, defined through dominance between sets of outcomes, has largely resisted characterization. A key contribution is a generalization of the classic decomposition lemma: a structural breakdown normally used to compare stable matchings now extends to any two outcomes from the same internally stable set. This exposes a tight connection between internal stability and the market’s cyclic architecture. Building on this characterization, we pinpoint the relations that matter for dominance stability and construct a reduced environment that concentrates all undominated outcomes. The main result: the vNM-stable set is unique and equals the core of this reduced environment. We thus obtain a structural characterization and a constructive method for computing it with standard matching-theory tools.

Context

Eight hundred participants, each with a strict preference list, enter a two-sided matching market. Some are firms. Others are workers. Everyone wants a single contract. Mathematically, this is a game theory with no repeat partnerships. The analysis of such markets began with John von Neumann and Oskar Morgenstern, who introduced the stable set concept. Yet linking the classical von Neumann–Morgenstern solution to modern matching theory tools proved tricky. The central question—can the vNM stable set be described in familiar terms?—long remained open. This work delivers a clear answer.

Methods

The path to the answer runs through two independent reductions of the preference profile. The first uses the optimization structure of the core. The core forms a lattice. Two extreme distributions—optimal for firms and for workers—set its boundaries. By sequentially removing pairs incompatible with moves inside the lattice (the Irving and Leather 1986 procedure), the authors keep only those relations that don’t break the dominance structure. The second reduction relies on the efficiency-adjusted deferred acceptance mechanism (EADA; Kesten, 2010). It filters out “blocking pairs”—those that stand in the way of Pareto improvements. Combining the two procedures yields the reduced profile P*. This is a collective behavior mirror of dominance ties.

Results

The main result: the von Neumann–Morgenstern stable set is unique. It exactly coincides with the core of the reduced profile P*. The chosen coalitions club is precisely all undominated outcomes in the specially trimmed game. Behind this compact statement lies a powerful technical apparatus. A key role is played by a generalization of the classic decomposition lemma. Differences between any two matchings from an internally stable set organize into cycles—IS-cycles. These cycles drive the reduction. They guarantee that the core of P* is both internally and externally stable in the original game. As a consequence, any outcome outside the core of P* is dominated by some club member. Moreover, the path to the core takes at most three dominance steps.

Implications

The result blurs the line between cooperative and structural views of stability. Instead of enumerating subsets, a simple procedure emerges: remove inessential pairs and take the core. This yields not only theoretical clarity. A practical algorithm appears. The notion of vNM stability ceases to be “exotic.” It slots into the general toolkit of matching theory. Dominance relations are now tied to topology.

Future development

The natural next step is to transfer the method to many-to-one and many-to-many markets. The lattice reduction generalizes partially. The EADA mechanism will need adaptation. Another direction is dynamics. Starting from an arbitrary state, how can agents drift toward the core? The three-step bound suggests: decentralized process converges fast. In the end, hundreds of participants can relax. Their chosen coalitions club is predictable.

Impact

Beyond pure game theory, the results matter for Dynamical Systems with decentralized decision making. Examples include organ donor allocation, school choice, and Graphs. Characterizing the stable set via a trimmed profile serves as a bridge between static normative analysis and realistic decentralized dynamics.

Next steps

Next steps: (1) generalize the characterization to multilateral markets; (2) build algorithms that find P* in polynomial time; (3) investigate whether decentralized dynamics always reach the core in exactly three steps.

Key open problems

The work contributes to the fundamental question of the existence of vNM stable sets in cooperative games. For matching markets, the answer is found: the set always exists and is unique. The reduction method shows that the key to the solution lies in removing inessential deviations.

🎯 The decomposition lemma, usually proved only for the core, is generalized here for the first time to any internally stable sets. It is this generalization that allows “slicing” the market into independent IS-cycles and building the reduction. Thus the chosen coalitions club is born.

Frobenius Carousel: A Fast Algorithm for Finding Normal Bases

Original: "Fast Deterministic Normal Bases and Circulant Polynomial Determinants"
· Mark Giesbrecht, Armin Jamshidpey, Éric Schost
arXiv:2607.00313 · 2026-07-01 · CC BY · 3 min
cs.SC Computational Complexity
A deterministic algorithm constructs a normal element of a finite field in near-quadratic time by reducing the problem to computing the determinant of a circulant matrix.
Abstract

We consider the deterministic search for a normal element in a degree‑n extension of a finite field. The central idea: the element β_t = (θ - t)^{-1} with parameter t ∈ F_q is normal for all t except at most n(n-1) values. Normality is captured by the non‑vanishing determinant of a circulant matrix—first a “Moore‑cleaned” version over F_{q^n}[T], then, for efficiency, an equivalent Gram matrix over F_q[T]; the determinant’s degree ≤ n(n-1). The key algorithmic innovation is a fast determinant evaluation for an n×n circulant matrix over F_q[t] (entries of degree ≤ m) via projections onto triangular sets and modular composition, achieving O_ϵ((nm log q)^{1+ϵ}) bit operations. When the field is too small (q < n(n-1)), the problem is lifted to a small‑degree extension with polylogarithmic overhead. The final algorithm finds a normal element in O_ϵ((n^2 log q)^{1+ϵ}) + O~(n log^2 q) bit operations.

Context

A finite field of q elements is a discrete universe where arithmetic follows the rules of division with remainder by a prime. In its extensions, like a multi-level labyrinth, elements can be represented in different ways. A normal basis is a special representation where the Frobenius automorphism (raising to the q-th power) acts as a cyclic shift of coordinates. This carousel of conjugates was already described by Gauss in his arithmetical investigations, but deterministic construction remained laborious: classical algorithms either multiplied matrices or relied on random luck.

Methods

The new construction starts from the family β_t = (θ−t)^{−1}, where θ is the image of the variable in the extension. Using the Moore determinant and a transition to a circulant matrix, the authors build a polynomial D(T) = det Circ(g_0(T),…,g_{n-1}(T)) over the base field. Its roots precisely correspond to the “bad” t that make β_t non-normal. For fast computation of D(T), they developed a circulant determinant algorithm, relying on triangular set techniques and modular composition (Kedlaya–Umans). The essence: the matrix is reduced to a characteristic polynomial in an algebra with a Gröbner basis, and p-adic lifts circumvent division by zero in Leverrier's formulas.

Results

The main result is a deterministic algorithm with complexity O_ϵ((n^2 log q)^{1+ϵ}) bit operations for any ϵ>0. By comparison, previous methods juggled n×n matrices, requiring either O(n^3) or O(n^ω) with ω≈2.37. Now practically all the time is spent on reading the input and writing the answer. For small fields where q < n(n−1), the algorithm extends the field to size q^ℓ with ℓ=O(log n), constructs a normal element there, and pulls it back — all deterministically. It is also proved that the determinant of an n×n circulant matrix with polynomials of degree m can be computed in O_ϵ((nm log q)^{1+ϵ}) bits — a result of independent value.

Implications

The result fills a long-standing need for deterministic fast initialization of normal bases. This is important for cryptographic protocols with guaranteed security and for computer algebra systems (SageMath, NTL), where rolling the dice is unacceptable. Moreover, the fast circulant determinant is a new tool for symbolic computation and coding theory.

Future development

The bottleneck is the projection of powers in the triangular basis; speeding it up to truly linear time would immediately improve the overall complexity to Õ(n^2). A second direction is extending the technique to non-cyclic extensions and to constructing a complete normal basis (all n conjugate elements) without additional cost.

Impact

The method directly accelerates q-th power operations in cryptographic libraries and applications involving polynomial factorization and representation isomorphism of finite fields.

Next steps

Integrate the algorithm into open-source computer algebra packages and conduct experiments on realistic field sizes (n≈10^3–10^4, q up to 2^256) to assess the practical gain over probabilistic counterparts.

Key open problems

The problem is directly connected to the efficient isomorphism problem: can one switch between two extensions of the same degree faster than quadratic time? A fast deterministic normal basis is one key to the answer, and progress here could influence the entire theory of finite fields.

Genome of an Aperiodic Crystal: Algebra Reveals Hidden Order

Original: "HK and GL"
· Hiroki Matui
arXiv:2607.00577 · 2026-07-01 · CC BY · 4 min
math.OA
A mathematical toolkit, akin to a genetic code, describes all possible electronic states in materials without periodicity.
Abstract

This paper studies the HK conjecture and gap-labelling for transformation groupoids arising from free actions of poly-ℤ groups on Cantor sets. The key tool: comparing long exact sequences in groupoid homology and cohomology with the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence for crossed products. New cohomological comparison maps, induced by K-theory classes of the group, team up with Poincaré duality to uncover higher homological summands crucial for HK. For ℤ, ℤ², and the Klein bottle group, both HK and gap-labelling are fully proven. For many groups with Hirsch length 3 and 4, the paper either proves HK outright or gives explicit exact sequences linking K-groups to homology and cohomology. Gap-labelling is tackled via the de la Harpe–Skandalis determinant, a trace formula for the Pimsner–Voiculescu boundary map, and transposition decompositions in topological full groups. The upshot: for all free poly-ℤ actions of Hirsch length 3, and for some of length 4 (including ℤ⁴), gap-labelling holds up to a factor of two.

1

Context

When electrons move through a periodic crystal, their allowed energies form bands—this is the ABC of solid-state physics. But what happens in quasicrystals, where the atomic pattern never repeats? There, the spectrum also has forbidden gaps, and the density of electronic states inside a gap is constant. The gap-labelling problem asks: which numbers can actually appear in these gaps? The answer must be encoded in the topology of the aperiodic pattern itself. About 40 years ago, Jean Bellissard showed that the key lies in the K-theory of C*-algebras and the homology of moduli spaces, but a precise algebraic description eluded researchers. Hiroki Matsui's new work offers a unified method that, like genome sequencing, allows us to read the 'instruction manual' for aperiodic order.

Methods

Matsui works with groupoids—generalizations of groups where multiplication isn't defined for all pairs, which perfectly captures the local symmetries of an aperiodic environment. Instead of directly computing K-groups, he builds bridges between them and homology and cohomology groups via exact sequences. The key technique is comparing the long homology sequence for semi-direct products with the Pimsner–Voiculescu sequence from K-theory, and introducing new cohomology comparison maps parameterized by K-theory classes of the acting group itself. Poincaré duality then allows interpreting the cohomological contributions as higher homologies. Anchor detail: the factor 1/2 in the gap-labelling estimate emerges because in topological full groups, any element of the index kernel decomposes into a product of transpositions—analogous to swapping two tiles in a mosaic.

Results

For actions of poly-Z-groups of small Hirsch length—1, 2, 3, and 4—exact descriptions of K-groups are obtained. In the one- and two-dimensional cases, the full HK conjecture (that the direct sum of homologies is isomorphic to K-theory) and exact gap-labelling are confirmed elementarily. For three-dimensional actions—both orientation-preserving and reversing—the injectivity of canonical homology maps is established, and gap-labelling is proved up to a factor of 1/2. In particular, for the Z³ action, the integral HK conjecture is rigorously proven for the first time. For the Kleinian group and some four-dimensional cases, the K₀-group decomposes into the sum of H₀ and H₂, with the H₄ summand splitting off as a direct factor. For Z⁴ and Z⁵, gap-labelling holds with the same factor 1/2. The method also revealed that the obstruction to full gap-labelling always has order 2—a consequence of the hidden structure of the topological full group.

Implications

This work provides, for the first time, a systematic tool for simultaneously studying the HK conjecture and gap-labelling via exact sequences. It clarifies why rational versions of the HY conjecture hold widely while integral ones do not: the obstructions have torsion, often of order 2. This sheds light on a long-standing dispute about the correctness of gap-labelling proofs for dimensions above two and provides an explicit criterion: gap-labelling in integers holds if the homology image is 2-divisible.

Future development

The next goal is to eliminate the factor 1/2. This may require a more refined analysis of the de la Harpe–Skandalis determinant or bringing in the Proitti–Yamashita spectral sequence. Another direction is to transfer the technique to groupoids that are not almost finite, and to actions of groups with torsion. In the long term, the method could help classify aperiodic patterns themselves by their 'genome'—the algebraic portrait.

Impact

The results will impact mathematical solid-state physics (quasicrystals, disordered systems), noncommutative geometry, and operator algebra theory. They may also clarify the nature of edge states in topological insulators.

Next steps

Test the HK conjecture for poly-Z-groups of Hirsch length 5 preserving orientation, and for wider classes of automorphisms. Investigate gap-labelling in cases where the homology image is not 2-divisible.

Key open problems

The paper directly addresses two fundamental open problems: the HK conjecture (linking K-theory and groupoid homology) and the gap-labelling problem (describing the density of states in a gap). Both hinge on understanding torsion in integral K-theory and homology.

🎯 The HK conjecture was first formulated by Matsui himself in 2012. In 2025, Scarparo and Deeley independently constructed counterexamples—groupoids for which it fails, reminiscent of finding 'genetic mutations' in the world of aperiodic systems. However, for natural classes such as Zⁿ actions on Cantor sets, it remains largely true.

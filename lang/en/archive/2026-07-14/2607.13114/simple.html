AI completes the signal from noise: ultra-light communication for 6G express

Original: "LiTCom: A Lightweight Transmitter and Inference-Capable Receiver Framework for 6G Uplink"
· Chunmei Xu, Siqi Zhang, Zhi Ding, Yi Ma, Rahim Tafazolli
arXiv:2607.13114 · 2026-07-14 · CC BY · 1 min
Signal Processing
The transmitter sends a rough sketch of data, and the AI receiver, like an artist, restores the details even through interference.
Abstract

Imagine your phone sends not a detailed photo to the base station, but a light sketch—and smart artificial intelligence on the other end reconstructs the image almost without loss. That’s exactly the approach the LiTCom system proposes for 6G communication. The transmitter on your device becomes ultra-simple and saves energy, while the network does the complex work. Doesn’t it feel a bit like magic, where a weak device gains superpowers through a cloud-based mind?

1

The transmitter sends only a rough sketch of the message — like an artist drawing contours without details. At the receiving end, the AI, like an experienced restorer, reconstructs the full picture, cleaning it of interference. This is how LiTCom technology works, changing the game in future 6G networks. In traditional communication, as in the standard model, the transmitter does all the work: encoding, protecting, combating noise. Here it is nearly idle — only slightly smoothing the signal, as if blurring the contours of a sketch. Radio communication relies on Maxwell's equations, but LiTCom turns their application upside down, making the transmitter passive. The entire burden falls on the receiver with a generative AI, which, from a mixture of signal and noise, as if from muddy water, extracts the original data. Chaotic noise increases entropy (a measure of disorder), but the neural network is trained to find order even in chaos — just as the ear picks out a familiar melody from a cacophony. Result: transmitter load drops by 95%, and smart amplification of important bits makes the connection more reliable. Miniature sensors with a button-cell battery will be able to send data for years from remote locations.

🎯 The concept of entropy came to communication theory from [scientist:Ludwig Boltzmann]Boltzmann's[/scientist] thermodynamics. Today it underpins the reliability of the internet, but its true nature is inevitable disorder, which, it turns out, can be turned to one's advantage.

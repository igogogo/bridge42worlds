A Single Drone Control System: From Search to Target Capture express

Original: "Autonomous Tracking and Terminal Guidance of Moving Targets for Fixed-Wing UAVs"
· Wei-Hao Liou, Teng-Hu Cheng
arXiv:2607.12801 · 2026-07-14 · CC BY · 1 min
Robotics Artificial Intelligence cs.SY eess.SY
A single algorithm, like a falcon's instinct, guides a drone with a pivoting camera from search to precise target capture.
Abstract

A control system has been developed for fixed-wing drones equipped with a camera on a gimbal. It handles the full mission cycle: target detection via the YOLO neural network, tracking with nonlinear model predictive control (NMPC), and final interception using biased proportional navigation (BPNG). To keep the drone from losing sight of the target due to body occlusion, the tracking algorithm incorporates control barrier functions (CBFs)—they ensure the camera always keeps the object in view. Simulations confirmed stable tracking and a precise hit at the desired angle.

To make the drone act like a predator, engineers wove three hunting phases into one: search, track, and strike. No more task-switching—its unified logic runs seamlessly.

At its heart is a mathematical filter designed for guiding Apollo spacecraft. It predicts the target's path, smoothing out jerks and interference, like a falcon that already knows where its prey will bolt.

The camera, sharp as an eagle's eye, scans the scene, finding the target by contrast and brightness (just as a hawk spots a mouse in tall grass). To keep the frame unobstructed, the algorithm respects the field-of-view limits—much like a bird doesn't peer under its own wing. Once the target is centered, a precise closing maneuver engages, steering the drone in a perfect arc, as if plunging into a dive. This approach echoes astronomy’s planet transiting a star, except here the object crosses a control zone amid the flight dynamics.

🎯 YOLO, the object detector, processes video so swiftly that the machine’s response outpaces the human eye. Even a smartphone can become the brain of an aerial hunter.

🎬 Unlike the relentless machines of ‘Terminator,’ this algorithm is built for rescue: finding the missing, inspecting pipelines, monitoring wildfires. A hunter, but with a noble cause.

A Smart Sieve for Search: How C3R Sifts Out Lies and Stays Silent in Doubt express

Original: "Certified Domain Consistency for Multi-Domain Retrieval: Label-Free Per-Domain Contamination Control with Conformal Risk Guarantees"
· Jayakumar Manoharan
arXiv:2607.14157 · 2026-07-14 · CC BY · 1 min
Machine Learning Artificial Intelligence cs.IR cs.SY eess.SY
A new method guarantees clean search results by refusing to respond when error risk is high.
Abstract

When searching across multi-domain corpora, evidence that is relevant but from the wrong domain is poorly caught by metrics and weakly controlled by conformal risk bounds, especially for the hardest domains. C3R is a pluggable layer that, based on inferred posterior domain probability without query labels, certifies a per-domain contamination budget and refuses to answer if it's exceeded, guaranteeing risk reduction for challenging domains. It relies on a two-split scheme with risk-controlling prediction sets and a finite-sample bound on the transfer from inferred to true domain with an estimated gap; heterogeneous budgets are supported. Across a thousand resampled calibrations, C3R's certificate was never violated, while marginal control always fails on the worst domain; soft degradation retains higher recall than the best cascade. The method is reproducible on open testbeds, including a corpus of federal regulations, and LLM evaluation confirms a reduction in non-authoritative justifications. The layer is reranker-agnostic and deploys as a frozen add-on.

Searching online is a lot like sifting flour for baking: white particles can hide dark specks of dirt. This data contamination is an invisible impurity that spoils the outcome. C3R operates like a smart sieve: it not only filters out information junk but also knows when to stop if there's too much trash. Rather than risk errors, the algorithm honestly declines to answer.

The beauty is that the sieve doesn't need 'dirty' labels—it assesses purity on the fly and guarantees that impurities never exceed a safe limit.

Internally, it performs analysis based on spectroscopy: data is separated by source, like light into a rainbow, and entropy—a measure of disorder—is calculated. Astonishingly, the method even withstands deliberate attacks: if someone intentionally pollutes the sources, the guaranteed accuracy still holds. In the end, search becomes a trustworthy tool for doctors and lawyers, where any inaccuracy could be a matter of life and death.

🎯 Search engines process trillions of documents every day; just a 1% error rate produces billions of misleading results.

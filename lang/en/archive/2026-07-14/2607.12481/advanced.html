How Electric Vehicles Stabilize Grid Voltage express

Original: "Scenario-Free Uncertainty-Aware DLMP-Based Bilevel Coordination of EV Charging and Reactive Power Support in Distribution Networks"
· Arash Baharvandi, Duong Tung Nguyen
arXiv:2607.12481 · 2026-07-14 · CC BY · 1 min
eess.SY cs.SY
A new method makes EV charging cheap and reliable, despite the whims of sun and demand.
Abstract

A two-level optimization framework is developed for coordinating EV charging and reactive power provision in distribution networks based on distribution locational marginal prices (DLMPs). At the upper level, an EV aggregator minimizes charging costs by co-optimizing active and reactive power, while at the lower level, an energy management system performs economic dispatch with network constraints and computes DLMPs. To handle uncertainties in load and photovoltaic generation, a compact robust reformulation describes the net load with a 'normal minus beta' distribution, better capturing real-data asymmetry compared to Gaussian models. An exact lemma preserves the economic interpretation of DLMPs after applying KKT conditions and Big-M linearization. Charging stations also provide voltage support by operating at a non-unity power factor. Simulations on the IEEE 33-bus system demonstrate improved voltage reliability, effective coordination under uncertainty, and significantly lower computational complexity than conventional stochastic and robust methods.

The power grid is often compared to a water supply: current flows like water under pressure. Electric vehicles here are powerful pumps, capable not only of pumping water (charging) but also pushing back when needed, evening out the pressure. The trouble is, solar panels generate energy in surges—like a pump that gulps and then blasts with double force. People switch on appliances at random, and the grid faces uncertainty—a turbulent stream with unpredictable eddies.

A new method works like a coordinated control room. First, a charging service schedules based on prices, then a smart algorithm distributes the load so the pipes don't burst. It predicts the sun's whims more accurately: instead of a naive 'up or down' forecast, it uses a skewness map—solar dips happen more sharply and often than spikes. A clever mathematical shortcut speeds up calculations tenfold, keeping voltage normal even in worst-case scenarios. A single charging station for a dozen cars can now tame surges comparable to firing up a steel furnace. The grid doesn't just survive—it becomes more flexible, and the carbon footprint melts away thanks to smart feeding from solar energy.

🎯 Solar generation can plummet by 80% in seconds when a cloud covers the panels—on graphs, it looks like a diving whale, hence the name 'whale effect'.

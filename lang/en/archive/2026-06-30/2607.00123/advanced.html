A Rubber Ruler for Infinite Series: A New Stability Criterion

Original: "Operators on Orlicz sequence spaces and $$\Delta_2$$-fundamentality"
· David E. Edmunds, Jan Krejčí, Zdeněk Mihula, Luboš Pick
arXiv:2607.00123 · 2026-06-30 · CC BY · 2 min
math.FA
A simple condition on a decreasing sequence reveals when discrete maximal and averaging operators don’t break the Orlicz space.
Abstract

It is known that the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator is bounded in the Orlicz space L^A(R^n) if and only if the conjugate Young function Ã satisfies the Δ2 condition. The same condition characterizes the boundedness of the Hardy averaging operator on L^A(0,∞). This paper considers a discrete analogue of the problem, generalized to a general interpolation scheme. Characterizations of boundedness for discrete maximal and averaging operators in Orlicz spaces are obtained. Proving the main result required new methods: the concept of a Δ2-fundamental sequence is introduced and characterized through the upper limit of the ratio of consecutive terms. A dual statement for Copson-type operators is also established.

1

Context

Orlicz spaces generalize classical Lp spaces, letting each scale have its own stiffness—like a rubber ruler with non-uniform elasticity. In optimization of estimates for partial differential equations and variational method problems, it’s crucial to know when key operators (maximal, Hardy, Hilbert) are bounded on such spaces. The continuous case has long been studied, but the discrete world of sequences held a surprise.

Methods

To bypass the traps of discrete indices, the authors developed a special tool—the ∆⁰₂-fundamental sequence. This is a decreasing-to-zero test sequence: if for some Young function the ratios of its values at doubled and original terms remain bounded, then the function automatically satisfies the ∆₂-condition near zero. They employ techniques from spectral analysis of rearrangements, information-theoretic inequalities, and probabilistic tail estimates for distributions. Unlike continuous analogues, where one often invokes Fourier analysis or wavelet transforms, here combinatorial rearrangement invariants took center stage.

Results

The main theorem establishes six equivalent conditions for the boundedness of the discrete maximal operator M and the Hardy operator P on the Orlicz space ℓᴬ. Among them is the membership of the complementary Young function Ã in the class ∆⁰₂. The most unexpected result is the characterization of ∆⁰₂-fundamentality: a sequence {tₙ} ↘ 0 is fundamental if and only if limsup tₙ/tₙ₊₁ ≤ 2. The threshold constant 2 works regardless of the choice of Young function. For the Copson operator Q, boundedness is equivalent to A ∈ ∆⁰₂, while the discrete Hilbert transform requires ∆⁰₂ for both A and Ã.

Implications

The result completes the discrete theory of Orlicz spaces, but also uncovers a new phenomenon—fundamental sequences with a simple criterion. A universal “moderation test” for scaling emerges, independent of any specific Young function. This sheds light on the nature of critical exponents in sequential dynamical systems.

Future development

The notion of a fundamental sequence likely carries over to the continuous setting, where it may yield new sufficient conditions for the boundedness of singular integrals. It would be interesting to explore analogues for Calderón–Zygmund operators and Muckenhoupt weights.

Impact

Harmonic analysis on sequences, interpolation theory of operators, functional inequalities.

Next steps

Study the continuous analogue of ∆⁰₂-fundamentality; check whether the threshold 2 persists for functions on the line.

Key open problems

This work is directly related to the problem of characterizing the boundedness of singular integrals in Orlicz spaces—a long-standing open question going back to Calderón and Zygmund.

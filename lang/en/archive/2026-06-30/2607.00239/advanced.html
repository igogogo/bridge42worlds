Эстетическая ретушь научных визуализаций: что дозволено человеку и ИИ

Original: "May (A)I Beautify Your Visualization? Expert Judgments of Acceptable Aesthetic Alterations"
· Kalina Borkiewicz, Jixian Li, Joshua A. Levine, Katherine E. Isaacs
arXiv:2607.00239 · 2026-06-30 · CC BY · 1 min
cs.HC cs.GR
This article has not been translated into this language yet. It is already available in other languages — switch the language in the header.
Abstract

We surveyed 95 visualization experts — researchers, practitioners, and domain scientists — about their attitudes toward 15 types of transformations applied to 3D visualizations of natural phenomena. The changes ranged from presentation-level adjustments (lighting, camera position) to data-level edits (removing errors, filling gaps), performed by both humans and AI. The results show that a transformation’s acceptability hinges on its semantic intent, not whether it affects presentation or data. Certain types of edits are consistently rated as more permissible, and their relative ranking is largely independent of who performed them. Nevertheless, AI-generated versions of the same changes are invariably seen as less acceptable than identical human-made edits. The clear divide between neutral and sensitive transformations calls for embedding constraints and safeguards into design practices and AI-assisted visualization tools that reflect this distinction.

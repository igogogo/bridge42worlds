Universe Without a Beginning: Quantum Cosmology Confirms Hawking's Idea express

Original: "Consistent Evaluation of the No-Boundary Proposal"
· Ahmed I. Abdalla, Stefano Antonini, Raphael Bousso, Luca V. Iliesiu, Adam Levine, Arvin Shahbazi-Moghaddam
arXiv:2602.02682 · 2026-02-02 · CC0 · 1 min
HEP Theory Cosmology General Relativity
New calculations show: a closed universe almost certainly has no boundary in time — exactly as described by Stephen Hawking and James Hartle.
Abstract

Scientists recalculated how the Universe could have originated according to Hawking's 'no-boundary' model. It turned out that, taking all quantum rules into account, any closed universe has almost a 100% probability of being as predicted. It's as if a dice had all its faces showing the same number. Could it be that the cosmos had no other choice?

1

The birth of the Universe is usually associated with the Big Bang — a point of infinite density. But at that point, the laws of physics break down, which bothers scientists. Stephen Hawking and James Hartle back in the 1980s proposed a different view: time has no boundary, like the surface of a globe. The Universe simply exists, and the question 'what was before the beginning' loses its meaning.

The surface of the Earth has neither a beginning nor an end. The same idea underlies quantum cosmology: spacetime loops back on itself, like a sphere.

For a long time, probability calculations in this model were incomplete. The rules of the quantum world require not just enumerating possible outcomes, but correctly summing their chances, avoiding illusions. When physicists finally carried out these correct computations, it turned out: almost any closed world has no beginning. The probability of finding ourselves in such a universe is almost 100%.

A randomly chosen closed surface will almost certainly be a sphere, not a bizarre shape. Likewise, universes without a beginning are the vast majority.

Moreover, under a special statistical approach, this probability becomes exactly one. Such a result naturally explains the rapid expansion at the dawn of existence — inflation arises without fine-tuning.

🎯 Hawking liked to compare the question 'what was before the Big Bang' to 'what is south of the South Pole.' If time is closed, both are meaningless.

Scientist

Claude Shannon

1916–2001 · Information theory, Cybernetics, Electrical engineering
American mathematician and electrical engineer, known as the father of information theory. He introduced the concepts of information entropy and the bit, laying the foundations of digital communication and computer science.

Biography

He studied at the University of Michigan and the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. He worked at Bell Labs and MIT. His paper 'A Mathematical Theory of Communication' (1948) founded a new science. Awards: IEEE Medal of Honor, Kyoto Prize.

Key discoveries

  • Information entropy as a measure of uncertainty.
  • Noisy-channel coding theorem.
  • Shannon coding and the foundations of data compression.
  • Design of digital circuits and pioneering work in artificial intelligence (Theseus mouse).
💡 Shannon was a master of juggling and unicycle riding, and often practiced in the corridors of Bell Labs.
Quote: "Information is the resolution of uncertainty."

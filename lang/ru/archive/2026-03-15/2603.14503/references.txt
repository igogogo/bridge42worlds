A Construction of DARKCLUSTERS-15K
To construct our DARKCLUSTERS-15Kdataset, we mostly used the TNG300-1 subset
of the IllustrisTNG cosmological simulation [42]. This dataset spans a cubic volume
with a side length of 205,000 ckpc/h that includes both baryonic matter (visible matter:
stars, black holes and gas) and dark matter. TNG300-1 contains thousands of galaxy
clusters resolved at high mass and spatial resolution. Additionally, we used the SIMBA
simulations [11], which are similar in terms of contents, volume, and resolution, but
rely on different algorithms than IllustrisTNG. Our SIMBA test set allows us to evaluate
generalization of our method under different simulation settings.
The surface mass density maps Σ(θ) include both baryonic (visible) and dark matter,
and are measured in (1010M⊙/h)/(ckpc/h)2, i.e., in terms of 1010 times the mass of
the Sun M⊙ per comoving kiloparsec squared, all scaled by the Hubble parameter h. We
also release maps where both components have been separated to facilitate analysis. The
photometry information Pf (θ) is measured in janskys (Jy). Since all DARKCLUSTERS-
15Kimages are 512×512 pixels, each pixel corresponds to an area of 1.56 (ckpc/h)2
for images with a field of view of 100×100 arcseconds, and 7.87 (ckpc/h)2 in the case
of225×225arcseconds, for clusters atz L = 0.5.
Selection of galaxy clusters.TNG300-1 provides a friends-of-friends catalog of individ-
ual galaxy clusters within the simulation. For each identified cluster, we measure its total
gravitationally-bound mass (thevirialmass) and the corresponding enclosing radius (the
virial radius). We use a similar strategy for clusters from SIMBA, using their provided
catalogs to filter by total cluster masses. To construct our dataset, we select 560 galaxy
clusters with virial masses of at least 1013.5 times the mass of our Sun M⊙. Figure 7
shows the distribution of selected galaxy clusters by their virial masses and radii. As
noted in our introduction, our analysis focuses on clusters in the range 1013.5–14M⊙,
which form the majority of our dataset.
A.1 Parsing data from IllustrisTNG
Each of the 500 extracted galaxy clusters contains volumetric information on mass and
emitted light, represented as 3D particle clouds. In our work, we process this information
to obtain 25 samples per cluster, where each sample consists of four images: one surface
mass density map Σ(θ), and three simulated HST images Pf (Section 4.1). Below, we
describe the processing steps for each one.
Surface mass density mapsΣ(θ).For baryonic matter, we use the Coordinates
and Masses fields of gas, star and black hole particles. For dark matter, we use
the Coordinates and SubfindDensity fields together with the MassTable at-
tribute. This allows us to provide visible and dark matter components in DARKCLUSTERS-
15K, although in our work we use only their combined mass.
We generate 25 different 2D projections from each 3D particle cloud (Figure 2).
These correspond to 25 different viewing directions that are uniformly distributed over
the surface of an hemisphere enclosing the cluster, each defined by an unit line-of-sight
direction ri. In practice, each corresponds to a rotation of the particle cloud and a

Photometry mapsPf .We use Coordinates and GFM_StellarPhotometrics

First, we add background light using publicly available mosaics from other HST
captures.
Second, we add intracluster light (ICL), which corresponds to light from stars that
are not bound to the galaxy cluster. Following Gonzalez et al. [18], we model ICL using
two Sérsic source parametric models [48] fit to the brightest cluster galaxies. We use
SE++ [4] to do the fitting, then we create the Sérsic models and add the resulting light to
the image.
Finally, we simulate several strongly lensed light sources. Following the process
described in Section 4.1 and Figure 2, we use a Sérsic parametric model for each light
on the source plane S, and simulate the corresponding observed image on the lens plane
L, which we add to the photometry.
A.2 Parsing data from SIMBA
Our pipeline for the SIMBA simulations [11] follows a very similar approach. We extract
60 galaxy clusters and also generate 25 projections per cluster, resulting in a test set
of 1,500 samples. Just as with IllustrisTNG, each sample consists of one surface mass

density map Σ(θ) and three HST images Pf in identical units. The main differences
from the IllustrisTNG setup are:
Surface mass density mapsΣ(θ).While we also use the Coordinates and Masses
for gas, stars and black holes, we now use the Masses field for dark matter particles as

Photometry maps Pf .Since SIMBA does not directly provide stellar magnitudes,

calculate the final magnitudes.
B Training and inference
For training and inference, we normalize the surface mass density maps Σ to the [0,1]
range using a logarithmic transform followed by shift and scale operations. We apply
the same normalization, independently, to the photometry mapsP f .
Training follows default configuration provided by InverseBench [54].
During inference, the loss weights λk for our strong and weak lensing forward
operators in Equation 10 are set as follows. For strong lensing, the geometric and
photometric losses use λgeo
s ≈0.6·10 −2 and λimg
s ≈0.6·10 −3 respectively, which
puts both losses on roughly the same scale. For weak lensing, we use λw ≈0.05 .
Additionally, for the DAPS algorithm, we set the parametersτ≈2.6·10 −3 and learning
rate of 2.8·10 −6 over 100 steps. The rest of the parameters use the default values
from InverseBench [54]. All these values were chosen through parameter sweeps on a
small validation subset of DARKCLUSTERS-15Kexcluded from both training and final
evaluation.
C Light-traces-mass in DARKCLUSTERS-15K
Here we measure the extent to which the light-traces-mass assumption holds in our
DARKCLUSTERS-15Kdataset. There are several previous works [29, 37] that assume a
1:1 relation of mass to light, while our work incorporates a diffusion-based prior that
can learn complex relations. For this, we compute the Pearson Correlation Coefficient
(PCC) between the mass and photometry maps at the f= 125 band, which we also
use for our implementation of the method from Napier et al. [37] in Section 6.3. We
evaluate this metric on the three IllustrisTNG test subsets of DARKCLUSTERS-15K,
corresponding to galaxy cluster redshifts zL ∈ {0.2,0.5,1.0}, obtaining values of 0.58,
0.61 and 0.65 respectively. These results indicate a moderate correlation. One of the
main differences comes from the presence of massive clumps of dark matter which
do not interact with light. There are also noticeable discrepancies primarily caused by
background light, intracluster light (Section A.1) and the absence of the scaling relations
from Limousin et al. [33] (Section 6.3).

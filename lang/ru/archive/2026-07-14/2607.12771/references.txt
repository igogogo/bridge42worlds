Dataset Processing Details
FukuyamaBenchmark
FukuyamaBench was constructed from theAdvanced Organic Reaction Mechanismworkbook,
which provides paired reaction questions and reference mechanism solutions. The original
materials contain reaction schemes and hand-drawn mechanism figures rather than directly
machine-readable trajectories. We therefore first decomposed the source materials into
reaction images and mechanism images. For each reaction image, we extracted the reactants,
products, and reaction conditions into textual molecular representations. The extracted
reaction texts were then manually checked and corrected by chemistry domain experts to
ensure consistency with the original problem.
For the mechanism side, Gemini was used only as an assistant for extracting and formatting
information from the provided mechanism figures, rather than as an unconstrained generator
of benchmark ground truth. In this annotation setting, Gemini was given substantially richer
information than the models evaluated on the benchmark, including the reactant and product
SMILES/SMARTS, reaction conditions, and the full reference mechanism image. Based on
each question as a boundary, the parsing accuracy for Set A, B, and C was 63/78, 76/131,
and 34/110 respectively. Each elementary reaction step was further inspected by RDKit to
ensure step-wise atomic and electronic conservation as shown in Example 1. The resulting
mechanism trajectories were reviewed and corrected by domain experts. Subsequently, using
the generated mechanism trajectories as inputs,intermediate checkpoints for the pathway
& product prediction task and the pathway-only prediction task were derived according to
predefined coarse-grained rules (See Section ). Checkpoints were also verified by experts.
Finally, atom mapping was assigned using RxnMapper and verified by experts, so that
backbone atom correspondence and reactive sites could be traced across elementary steps as
shown in Example 2 and fig. 6. This process yielded atom-mapped trajectories with explicit
intermediates, products, reaction conditions, and checkpoint-level mechanistic states. The
27
benchmark was then organized into three tasks: step-wise only prediction, pathway-only
prediction, and pathway & product prediction. Importantly, during benchmark evaluation,
models are given only the task-specific inputs such as reactants/intermediate states and
reaction conditions; they are not given the product SMILES, SMARTS, or reference mechanism
images used during dataset construction.

Training Dataset
For data construction, we first transformed raw reaction strings to a reaction-wise trajectory
representation. Each reaction is stored as an ordered list of elementary steps with explicit
step index, reactants, products, and raw reaction string. This preserves causal order for
mechanism supervision. Identity transitions are removed during formatting, so only chemically
active steps are retained. We then perform pathway-level reasoning annotation, where each
sample starts from initial reactants and predicts the complete multi-step trajectory. The
30
Figure 6: Visualization of mapped RXN SMILES in B111-a.
annotator is constrained to produce both free-form forward reasoning and a structured result
JSON. To improve robustness at scale, we use bounded asynchronous generation, retry with
exponential backoff, periodic checkpointing, and automatic resume; invalid entries, such as
empty trajectories or abnormal reaction identifiers, are filtered before annotation. The valid
annotations are finally converted into a standard multi-turn instruction format for supervised
fine-tuning.

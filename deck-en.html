From “what we missed” to “what the field missed”

A blind spot is an empty region surrounded by density. That is not “impossible” — that is “nobody looked”. Our own pipeline had one: two years of collecting only astro-ph, a decision made once and then invisible. The map found it in a single pass.

The density wall — stated before anyone asks

Searching for voids directly in the embedding cannot work, and this is geometry, not budget. Intrinsic dimension (Levina–BickelHow intrinsic dimension is estimated. A maximum-likelihood estimator built from distances to the k nearest neighbours: if a point’s neighbours are arranged as they would be inside a d-dimensional ball, the ratios of those distances reveal d. Ours is stable at 32–35 for k from 10 to 80, which is why the figure can be argued about rather than merely quoted. MLE): ≈40. With 1.56M points that is 1.4 points per axis — every point is isolated at almost any radius. Nearest-neighbour distance scales as N−1/d: doubling the corpus shrinks it by 2%. Densifying twofold requires 234 ≈ 1.7 × 1010 times more papers than exist.

Controls are built into the instruments, not added later

Blind evaluation with a sealed key. Foreign-anchor controls in every cycle run. Known-positive calibration pairs mixed into every judging batch. Baselines printed next to every result — random half, random pair, foreign anchor. An instrument that answers always is treated as broken.

Honest boundary, and where the subsurface analogy breaks

In science an empty region means “not studied”. In exploration it often means “drilled and dry” — and that report sits in the archive. The system must distinguish not drilled from drilled without result, and negative results are the least documented data a company holds. This is settled before a pilot, not after.
